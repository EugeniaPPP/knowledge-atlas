<details>
<summary>Editorial adaptations and clarifications</summary>

This chapter is a self-contained textbook treatment based on the Yale materials. The political caricature at both ends of the scale is presented as Ben Polak's joke.

Mastery exercises 1–2 use a new six-position grid with 60 voters. Exercise 3 adapts the three-candidate extension in Problem Set 1; its numerical counterexample was calculated separately. Exercise 4 changes the probabilistic beliefs. The A/B/C own-payoff table in exercise 5 is an original editorial example. Exercise 6 checks all maximizers in the original model, including ties. The written explanation is assessed by the reader against a rubric, without an automatic score.

Editorial clarifications distinguish the median from the geometric center and the discrete result from other versions of the theorem. They also make explicit that expectations use the given numerical utilities, that ignorance does not establish equal probabilities, and that an intersection of two lines must be checked against the third.
